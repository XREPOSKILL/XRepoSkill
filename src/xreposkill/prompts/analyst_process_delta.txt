SWE-bench issue: one FAILED, one SUCCESSFUL, produced by different backbone
models running the same agent.

GOAL: Identify the earliest step in the successful trajectory where it took
an acquisition action that the failed trajectory did not take (or took
wrongly). Output the MISSING PROCESS STEP, not the resulting knowledge.

OUTPUT: a single JSON object:
{
  "kind": "action" | "fact",
  "category": "lookup" | "verification" | "replay" | "search_recipe",
  "rule": "<one paragraph of at most 1000 characters that states when to take the step and what the step is>",
  "predicate": "<DSL expression>" | null,
  "evidence": {
    "fail_traj_id": "<id>",                # required
    "pass_traj_id": "<id>",                # required
    "skipped_step_index": <int>,           # required: fail-side step where the divergence happens
    "cited_pass_step_index": <int>,        # required: pass-side step doing the right thing
    "gold_patch_hunk_ref": null
  }
}

Process-delta language is preferred for action rules:
  GOOD "The successful trajectory ran the failing test (step 3) before
       editing; the failed one began editing at step 2 without reproduction."
  GOOD "The successful trajectory grepped for callers of `_dispatch`
       (step 5) before changing its signature; the failed one changed it
       directly at step 4."

INPUT FORMAT: you receive a localized FORK, not whole trajectories:
  fork.shared_prefix_events   aligned events both sides performed
  fork.fail_divergence_step / fork.pass_divergence_step
                              absolute step index where each side diverges
  fork.fail_divergence_excerpt / fork.pass_divergence_excerpt
                              the steps from the divergence point onward
  fork.fail_continuation_events / fork.pass_continuation_events
                              compact event views of the two continuations
The divergence point IS the decision fork: state what the successful side
did there that the failed side did not. Cite step indices from the
excerpts (they are absolute).
